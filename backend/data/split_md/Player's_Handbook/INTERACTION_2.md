# Interaction

Exploring dungeons, overcoming obstacles, and slaying
monsters are key parts of D&D adventures.
No less
important,
though, are the social interactions
that
adventurers
have with other inhabitants
of the world.
Interaction
takes on many forms. Vou might need
to convince an unscrupulous
thief to confess to some
malfeasance,
or you might try to fialter a dragon so that
it will spare your life. The DM assumes
the roles of any
characters
who are participating
in the interaction that
don't belong to another player at the table. Any such
character
is called a nonplayer
character
(NPC).
In general terms, an NPC's altitude toward you is
described
as friendly, indifferent, or hostile. Friendly
NPCs are predisposed
to help you, and hostile ones are
inclined to get in your way. It's easier to get what you
want from a friendly NPC, of course.
Social interactions
have two primary aspects:
roleplaying and ability checks.
